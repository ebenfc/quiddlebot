# Quiddler rules reference and house conventions

Paraphrased for quick lookup. The rule sheet in the box is the source of truth.

## Game structure
- Eight hands. Hand 1 deals 3 cards each; every later hand deals one more, up to 10 cards in hand 8.
- 1 to 8 players. Shuffle before every hand; the deal passes to the left.
- Each turn: draw from the pile or take the top discard, then discard one card.
- To go out: arrange every card but one into words, lay them down, discard the last card.
- After someone goes out, every other player gets one final turn and lays down what they can.
- You cannot add to or play on another player's words.

## Words
- Every word uses at least two cards.
- Not allowed: proper nouns, prefixes, suffixes, abbreviations, hyphenated words.
- Agree on a dictionary before the game. Look things up only when it is not your turn.

## Scoring a hand
1. Add the point values of every card in your words.
2. Subtract the point values of cards you could not use.
3. **A hand never scores below zero.** Players miss this one for years.
4. Bonuses: +10 for the longest word, +10 for the most words. Both can go to one player.
   The longest word is counted in letters, not cards, so a TH card counts as two letters.
   **If two or more players tie for a bonus, nobody gets it.**
5. Two players: use only one bonus. The rules don't say which; agree before the game.

## Challenges
- Challenge a word immediately after it is laid down.
- If the word is good, the challenger loses its point value. If not, the player who laid it
  loses its value and cannot rearrange the cards.
- A lost challenge is the only way a hand can end up negative.
- If the challenged player had gone out, play continues as if they went out.

## House conventions (our table)
- Default 2 players, sometimes 3 or 4. Eight rounds, hard cap.
- 3 or 4 players: both bonuses, ties cancel.
- 2 players: Longest Word only, unless Most Words is switched on at game start.
- Word disputes are settled with the dictionary we agreed on. The app's word check (v1.2) is a
  hint from a built-in list, not a ruling: it never changes a score.
